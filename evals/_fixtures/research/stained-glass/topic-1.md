# Findings - Topic 1: Making the coloured glass

## Passage 1

Colour was put into the glass while it was molten, by adding metal oxides to the batch. Cobalt gave blue and copper gave red. Red glass made this way was so dark it was almost black, so glaziers used "flashed" red instead: a sheet of clear glass dipped in or coated with a thin layer of red glass.

Evidence: verified

Sources:
- https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/medieval-stained-glass-colour (museum article, 2019)
- https://www.corpusvitrearum.example/technique/flashed-ruby (research project's technical glossary, undated)

Reliability: the glossary page carries no date.

## Passage 2

Pieces were cut by drawing a red-hot iron along a line marked on the glass, which made it crack along that line. The edge was then trimmed with a "grozing iron," a flat iron bar with a notch at its end used to nibble the glass to shape. Diamond cutters came into use much later.

Evidence: verified

Sources:
- https://www.medievalcraft-journal.example/2016/glass-cutting-methods (peer-reviewed article, 2016)

## Passage 3

Faces, folds of cloth, and lettering were painted onto the glass in a brown-black paint called grisaille, made of ground glass and iron oxide. The painted piece was fired in a kiln so the paint fused to the surface.

Evidence: reported

Sources:
- https://www.corpusvitrearum.example/technique/flashed-ruby (research project's technical glossary, undated)
- https://www.cathedral-trust.example/york/conservation/grisaille (cathedral conservation studio blog, 2021)

Reliability: the conservation blog describes its own studio's reconstructions, not medieval records.
