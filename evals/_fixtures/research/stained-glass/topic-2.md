# Findings - Topic 2: Building the window

## Passage 1

The cut and painted pieces were held together by strips of lead with an H-shaped cross-section, called cames. The glass sat in the channels of the H, and the joints between strips were soldered. A typical lancet window held about 800 pieces of glass.

Evidence: verified

Sources:
- https://www.stainedglassmuseum.example/collections/how-a-window-is-made (museum guide, 2020)
- https://www.archaeology-review.example/2018/chartres-lancet-survey (survey report of one cathedral's lancets, 2018)

Reliability: the survey covers one cathedral, so the piece count may not hold for others.

## Passage 2

Before any glass was cut, the glazier drew the design full size on a whitewashed wooden table. The pieces were cut and laid out over the drawing. When the panel was finished, the table was washed and whitened again for the next design.

Evidence: verified

Sources:
- https://www.medievalcraft-journal.example/2014/theophilus-glazing (peer-reviewed article on a 12th-century craft treatise, 2014)

## Passage 3

Iron bars, called armatures, ran across the window opening and carried the weight of the leaded panels.

Evidence: reported

Sources:
- https://www.stainedglassmuseum.example/collections/how-a-window-is-made (museum guide, 2020)
