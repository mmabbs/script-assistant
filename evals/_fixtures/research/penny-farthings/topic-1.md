# Findings - Topic 1: Penny-farthings in everyday use, and their riders

## Passage 1

In April 1908 the Coventry Evening Herald printed a photograph of Albert Rowe, a watchmaker, riding his penny-farthing to his workshop on Hertford Street. The caption said he rode it there every working day.

Evidence: reported

Sources:
- https://www.coventry-archives.example/newspapers/1908-04-evening-herald-rowe (digitized local newspaper, 1908)

Reliability: one photograph caption about one rider.

## Passage 2

The penny-farthing riders of these years were mostly men who had learned on the machine in their youth. The Ordinary Riders' Club, a club for riders of the old high-wheelers, held an annual run through Coventry. Its 1908 run had 14 riders, and their average age was 58.

Evidence: verified

Sources:
- https://www.cycling-history-journal.example/2012/the-ordinary-riders-club (peer-reviewed article, 2012)
- https://www.ordinary-riders-archive.example/gazette/1908-annual-run (digitized club gazette, 1908)

## Passage 3

Penny-farthings were everyday transport on English roads only from about 1875 to 1885. In the late 1880s the safety bicycle, with two wheels of the same size and a chain driving the back wheel, replaced them, and by 1892 no English maker still offered a high-wheeler in its catalogue.

Evidence: verified

Sources:
- https://www.cycle-heritage-museum.example/collections/from-high-wheeler-to-safety (museum collection article, 2017)

## Passage 4

In the United States in the same years, the few high-wheelers still ridden were mostly in circus and vaudeville acts. The Kellard Brothers, a troupe of trick riders, toured American theatres from 1907 to 1913 with an act on high-wheelers.

Evidence: reported

Sources:
- https://www.circus-history-review.example/2015/trick-cyclists-on-the-vaudeville-stage (journal article, 2015)
