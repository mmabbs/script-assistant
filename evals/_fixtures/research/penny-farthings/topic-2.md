# Findings - Topic 2: Riding a penny-farthing

## Passage 1

A rider mounted from behind. Holding the handlebars, he set his left foot on a small step fixed to the frame just above the back wheel, pushed off with his right foot to get the machine rolling, then stood up on the step and slid forward into the saddle above the front wheel. To get off, he did the same in reverse, finding the step with his left foot as the machine slowed.

Evidence: verified

Sources:
- https://www.ordinary-riders-archive.example/handbooks/1908-riding-the-ordinary (digitized club handbook, 1908)
- https://www.cycle-heritage-museum.example/collections/riding-the-high-wheeler (museum collection article, 2017)

## Passage 2

The rider sat almost directly above the hub of the front wheel. A stone or a rut that stopped the wheel could throw him over the handlebars head first, a fall riders called "taking a header." The 1908 handbook told riders to hook their legs over the handlebars when going down steep hills, so that a fall would land them feet first.

Evidence: verified

Sources:
- https://www.ordinary-riders-archive.example/handbooks/1908-riding-the-ordinary (digitized club handbook, 1908)
