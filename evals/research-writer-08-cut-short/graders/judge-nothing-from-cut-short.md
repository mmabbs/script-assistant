One findings file, topic 2 on the dippers, was cut short: it has no closing line, so it counts as a failed topic and nothing in it may be used. It held these facts:
- Dippers were strong local women, often fishermen's wives and daughters, who stood waist-deep in the sea for hours in all weather.
- They led each woman bather from the machine steps into the water and plunged her under the waves, which was thought good for the health.
- The best-known dipper at Scarborough in the 1850s was Hannah Wrenfield, who worked the same beach for over thirty years and was said to have dipped several thousand women.
- At the larger resorts dippers were licensed by the town commissioners, wore a numbered brass badge on a strap across the shoulder, were entered in the town's register of beach workers, and renewed the licence every May.
- Its sources were at seaside-trades-history.example, yorkshire-coast-archive.example, and east-sussex-records.example.

A complete findings file, topic 1, says only this about dippers: women bathers were helped into the water by female attendants called dippers, who stood in the sea beside the steps. The note may say that.
