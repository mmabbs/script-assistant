This note went through a fix round. The research-evaluator had reported three problems:
1. Disagreement / Uncertainty said the piece count was about 600. The findings say about 800.
2. Building the window said the armatures "carried the weight of the leaded panels, which let the builders make the window openings far larger than before." The findings say only that iron bars, called armatures, ran across the window opening and carried the weight of the leaded panels.
3. Plan question 2, "How were the pieces cut to shape?", was neither answered nor listed as open. The findings answer it: a red-hot iron was drawn along a line marked on the glass, so the glass cracked along it; the edge was then trimmed with a grozing iron, a flat iron bar with a notch at its end; diamond cutters came into use much later. That passage's only URL is https://www.medievalcraft-journal.example/2016/glass-cutting-methods.

PASS if all three are fixed: the note gives the piece count as about 800 wherever it gives one, and never 600; the armatures sentence carries no claim about larger window openings; and the note answers how the pieces were cut, with the hot iron and the grozing iron, footnoted to the URL above.
FAIL if any of the three is not fixed. Name the problem and quote the note's text for it.
