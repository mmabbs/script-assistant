# Tide Tables

Request: How did printed tide tables reach small villages in the 1950s? (#research/run-2)

## Findings

- Small villages got their tide tables from a printed almanac sold at the chandler's, a new one each January.
- The harbour office copied the week's tides onto a slate beside the pier for anyone who could not afford one.
- Almanac times were for the nearest standard port, so a village had to add or subtract a local correction.
- Fishing families wrote their own corrections in the margin and handed the book down.
- Keepers received a separate table from the Board, printed on card and corrected for their own rock.

## Useful for the story

- Mara's timing of the causeway is a local correction she keeps in her head, not in any printed table.
- The slate by the pier is a place the village gathers, and a place the relief keeper can be seen being new.

## Open questions

- How often the harbour office slate was wrong. No source says.

## Sources

- Carrick Bay chandler's price list, 1954.
- Harbour office notice book, 1949 to 1961.
