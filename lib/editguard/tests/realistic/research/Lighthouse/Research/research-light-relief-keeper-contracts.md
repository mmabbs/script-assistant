# Relief Keeper Contracts

Request: What did a relief keeper's contract look like in the 1950s? (#research/run-2)

## Findings

- Relief keepers were engaged for a season at a time, usually six months, and could be moved between stations without notice.
- The contract named a lodging allowance, a coal ration, and a clause forbidding the station to be left unattended.
- A relief keeper reported to the principal keeper, not to the Board. Only the principal could file the quarterly report.
- Pay was about two thirds of a principal keeper's, with no pension for service under five years.
- Relief keepers were often boys from fishing families, because the Board wanted people used to the water.

## Useful for the story

- The reporting line gives Mara real power over him, and a reason to be hard on him that is not only temper.
- The no-pension clause explains why he arrives with everything he owns in one bag.
- The unattended-station clause is the rule Mara breaks in act three.

## Open questions

- Whether a principal keeper could ask the Board to send someone else. The sources do not say.
